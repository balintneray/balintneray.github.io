<a name="7-methods-playbook"></a>
# 7. Methods Playbook — Senior-level framings

For each method: when to use it, sample question, pitfall, and how a senior frames it vs. a junior.

### 7.1 Generative / foundational / discovery research
**Use when:** problem space is fuzzy, leadership has a hunch, you're entering a new domain. **Don't use when:** scope is locked or you need go/no-go in two weeks.

**Sample Q:** *"What does shopping with AI agents look like for Target guests?"* / *"Where does household decision-making actually happen?"*

**Pitfalls:** boiling the ocean, no decision-owner, sample of convenience.

**Senior framing:** Junior plans interviews; senior negotiates the *learning agenda* with stakeholders upfront, scopes to decisions, mixes methods, delivers *problem framing* (jobs, tensions, opportunity areas) — not a transcript dump. Names what's out of scope.

### 7.2 Concept testing & desirability (WoZ, video sketches, smoke tests, fake-door, A/B concept)
**Use when:** 2–4 directional bets and need desirability/comprehension signal before committing eng.

- **Wizard-of-Oz:** test AI/agentic flows before the model works
- **Video sketches:** sell the vision and probe reactions to a future state
- **Smoke tests / fake-door:** measure interest in unbuilt features (ethically — redirect to "coming soon" with opt-in)
- **A/B concept:** comparative reactions to framings, not pixel-perfect designs

**Sample Q:** *"Does a proactive AI shopping assistant feel helpful or intrusive at the aisle?"* / *"Which framing of an 'AI-picked basket' drives sign-up intent?"*

**Pitfalls:** confusing desirability with usability; leading stimuli; treating fake-door clicks as commitment; not capturing the *why*.

**Senior framing:** Design the *concept portfolio* so results actually differentiate bets; call out construct validity ("we're measuring novelty, not intent"); pair quant signal with 6–8 interviews for the why.

### 7.3 Longitudinal / diary studies
**Use when:** behavior unfolds over time (meal planning, AI tool adoption); trust/habit formation matters.

**Sample Q:** *"How does a guest's trust in an AI list-builder evolve over 4 weeks?"*

**Pitfalls:** fatigue, drop-off, recall bias.

**Senior framing:** Design incentive cadence; instrument analytics in parallel to triangulate self-report against behavior; structure synthesis around *trajectories* (not aggregate themes).

### 7.4 In-store intercepts, shop-alongs, contextual inquiry, ethnography
**Use when:** context drives behavior (cart, kids, aisle, signage, phone in hand) — critical for Target.

- **Intercepts:** 5–10 min; breadth
- **Shop-alongs:** 45–90 min; depth, surfaces workarounds
- **Ethnography:** in-home, multi-visit; the system around shopping (pantry, list, family negotiation)

**Sample Q:** *"How do guests reconcile their digital list with what they actually buy in-aisle?"*

**Pitfalls:** Hawthorne effect; store-associate interference; legal/PR risk; recruiting bias at entrance (skews to non-rushed).

**Senior framing:** Partner with store ops + legal *before* fieldwork; build recruiting to capture missed segments (parents with strollers, Drive-Up users); frame findings as *system insights* — "the cart-phone-list triangle is broken at these three moments."

### 7.5 Research on AI / agentic experiences
**Frameworks:** trust calibration (predictability, transparency, control, reliability); mental-model elicitation; recovery & repair journeys; hallucination tolerance by stakes.

**Always evaluate:** (1) does the user know what the agent *can* and *can't* do, (2) can they detect errors, (3) can they recover, (4) does trust calibrate over time (under- or over-trust)?

**Methods that work:** WoZ for pre-build agents; structured "break-it" sessions; think-aloud + retrospective; longitudinal trust tracking; LLM-as-judge for eval at scale paired with human spot-checks.

**Sample Q:** *"Where do guests over-trust the AI's product recommendation vs. verify?"* / *"When the agent hallucinates, how do guests notice and recover — and does it damage trust in the brand?"*

**Pitfalls:** demoing the happy path only; ignoring error/recovery; conflating "delight" with "trust"; failing to test with low-AI-literacy guests.

**Senior framing:** Treat the AI as a *system*, not a feature. Research the seams (handoff to human, fallback, escalation). Define evals jointly with DS/ML. Bring a point of view on responsible disclosure and brand risk.

### 7.6 Research for high-fidelity storytelling & prototype-driven vision-selling
**Use when:** the team's job is to make an internal audience *believe* a future is possible. Research's role: ground the prototype in real tensions, then measure resonance.

**Sample Q:** *"Which moments in this vision film make execs lean in vs. tune out?"* / *"Does the prototype solve a tension guests actually feel, or one we invented?"*

**Pitfalls:** research as set-dressing for a pre-decided concept; pretty videos with no insight provenance.

**Senior framing:** Pair *outward* research (resonance with guests) with *inward* research (which exec objections will kill this, and how do we pre-empt). Treat the vision artifact as a *probe* — every screening is a research session.

### 7.7 Quant/qual triangulation & data-science partnership
**Use when:** strategic questions needing both "how many" and "why." Design methods to interrogate each other, not run serially.

**Sample Q:** *"We see a 12% drop-off at step 3 — what's the story, and is it one cohort or all?"*

**Pitfalls:** surveys without qual grounding; DS getting research as deliverable instead of partner; p-hacking themes.

**Senior framing:** Co-own metric definitions with DS/PM upfront. Push back on "let's just A/B it" when the construct is novel or trust is involved. Read SQL/dashboards well enough to spot wrong-question quant.

### 7.8 Research for systems — blueprints, journeys, ecosystem maps
**Use when:** experience crosses channels (app → store → fulfillment → return), roles (guest, associate, driver), or time.

**Sample Q:** *"Where in the Drive-Up service blueprint does AI add value vs. add friction for associates?"*

**Pitfalls:** beautiful blueprints nobody uses; mapping current state and calling it done; ignoring the employee/associate side.

**Senior framing:** Use the artifact to drive *org alignment*. Run working sessions where ops, tech, and design co-edit the blueprint. Map *moments of truth* (trust made/broken) and *cross-functional ownership gaps*. Always pair future-state with current-state so the delta is visible.

# 8. STAR Story Worksheet

Fill each scaffold with a *real* story. Aim for 8–10 distinct stories total — most repurpose across 2–3 questions. Tag each with **Care / Grow / Win Together**.

### 8.1 Thriving in ambiguity
**Q:** *"Tell me about a time you were handed a vague, undefined problem space. How did you structure it?"*
- **Tests:** comfort without a brief; ability to scope, sequence, create clarity
- **S:** the prompt, who handed it to you, what was missing
- **T:** what you decided the *real* question was
- **A:** how you decomposed it (assumption mapping, stakeholder interviews, research plan v0)
- **R:** clarity / decision / artifact the team walked away with
- **Probes:** "What did you deprioritize?" / "How did you know you'd scoped it right?"

### 8.2 Research influencing a strategic decision
**Q:** *"Describe research that changed a product or business direction — not just a feature."*
- **Tests:** strategic altitude; influence beyond usability
- **S:** the strategic bet on the table
- **T:** your role in informing it
- **A:** method choice, who you involved, how you packaged for execs
- **R:** decision made, dollars/roadmap/headcount shifted, downstream impact
- **Probes:** "Who pushed back?" / "How did you measure it was the right call?"

### 8.3 Disagreement with a senior stakeholder
**Q:** *"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a VP or director."*
- **Tests:** backbone + diplomacy; data-grounded conviction
- **S:** disagreement, stakes, who was in the room
- **T:** what you believed was right and why
- **A:** how you raised it (private first? data-led? reframed?)
- **R:** outcome — include what you *conceded*, not just what you won
- **Probes:** "What would you do differently?" / "How is the relationship now?"

### 8.4 Mentoring another researcher (***hits Julian's servant-leader signal***)
**Q:** *"How have you grown a more junior researcher?"*
- **Tests:** "Grow" value; senior-level multiplier behavior
- **S:** who they were, growth edge
- **T:** what you committed to helping them develop
- **A:** specific rituals (1:1s, shadowing, feedback on readouts, stretch assignments)
- **R:** concrete progression — promotion, ownership of a study, confidence shift
- **Probes:** "What didn't work?" / "How did you adapt your style?"

### 8.5 Quant + qual with data science
**Q:** *"Walk me through a study where you partnered closely with data science."*
- **Tests:** mixed-methods fluency; respect for another craft
- **S:** the question that needed both signals
- **T:** division of labor — what only quant vs. only qual could answer
- **A:** how you sequenced (qual to shape hypotheses? quant to size? triangulation?)
- **R:** integrated insight, decision made, relationship built
- **Probes:** "When did the two methods disagree?" / "Who owned the final narrative?"

### 8.6 Researching AI / emerging tech (***directly aligns with the JD***)
**Q:** *"Tell me about research you've done on AI or an emerging technology where mental models were unclear."*
- **Tests:** comfort researching the unfamiliar; future-facing posture
- **S:** the tech and what was unknown about user reception
- **T:** the research question (trust? comprehension? value? hallucination tolerance?)
- **A:** method adaptation — WoZ, concept testing, longitudinal diary?
- **R:** what shipped, what got killed, frameworks you left behind
- **Probes:** "How did you handle participant misconceptions about the tech?" / "Ethical guardrails?"

### 8.7 In-store / contextual / ethnographic (***directly aligns with the JD***)
**Q:** *"Tell me about field or in-context research you led."*
- **Tests:** Target's core — guest experience often lives in stores
- **S:** the environment, why remote/lab wouldn't have worked
- **T:** what behavior you needed to observe vs. what people *say*
- **A:** recruiting, logistics, observation protocol, in-the-moment adaptation
- **R:** behavioral insight that contradicted stated preference; what it changed
- **Probes:** "What surprised you?" / "How did you bring stakeholders into the field?"

### 8.8 Killing a project / changing direction
**Q:** *"Tell me about a time your research said 'don't build this'."*
- **Tests:** courage, business judgment, framing bad news constructively
- **S:** the project, momentum behind it, sunk cost
- **T:** what the data was telling you
- **A:** how you delivered — to whom, in what forum, with what alternative
- **R:** decision, resources reallocated, team morale handled
- **Probes:** "How did the build team react?" / "How did you preserve the relationship?"

### 8.9 Failure / do-over
**Q:** *"Tell me about a study that didn't go well."*
- **Tests:** self-awareness; learning posture (Target's "Grow")
- **S:** setup and what went sideways
- **T:** what you were trying to achieve
- **A:** what you actually did, including the misstep
- **R:** real impact + specific lesson + where you've applied it since
- **Probes:** "When did you realize it was off track?" / "What system prevents recurrence?"

### 8.10 Scaling research practice (***hits Julian's CXO-metrics signal***)
**Q:** *"How have you scaled research beyond your own studies?"*
- **Tests:** operating leverage; thinking like an org, not an IC
- **S:** bottleneck or gap in the practice
- **T:** what you decided to systematize (repo, intake, democratization, panel, ResearchOps, metrics framework)
- **A:** who you partnered with, how you piloted, how you got adoption
- **R:** adoption metric, time saved, decisions accelerated
- **Probes:** "What didn't get adopted?" / "How did you avoid lowering the quality bar?"

### 8.11 Cross-functional conflict (PM / design / eng)
**Q:** *"Tell me about navigating a conflict between PM, design, and engineering."*
- **Tests:** Win Together; researcher as neutral broker
- **S:** conflict and what each function wanted
- **T:** your role — not to take a side, but to surface the right question
- **A:** how you reframed via user evidence; what you ran to break the tie
- **R:** decision, alignment, and how the *relationship* changed
- **Probes:** "Whose side were you actually on?" / "What if research had been ambiguous?"

### 8.12 Care / Grow / Win Together in action
**Q:** *"Target's values are Care, Grow, Win Together. Give an example."*
- **Tests:** values alignment; pick the one your story most authentically demonstrates
- **A:** a *concrete behavior*, not a platitude (pulled someone into a readout; took a hit for the team; gave credit publicly)
- **Probes:** "Which do you most need to grow in?" / "How do you model this for others?"

## Rehearsal protocol
1. **Write before you speak.** Bullet S/T/A/R in one line each. If you can't, the story isn't ready.
2. **Time-box: 2–3 minutes per answer.** S+T: ~30s. A: ~90s (this is the meat). R: ~30s — land it hard.
3. **Quantify the Result.** Every story needs at least one number OR a named decision it caused.
4. **Record on voice memo.** Play back at 1.25x. Cut filler. Tighten verbs in A.
5. **Pressure-test with a partner.** Have them ask probes cold. Practice "what would you do differently" for *every* story.
6. **Build a 12×10 grid:** questions across the top, stories down the side. Mark coverage. Each story should cover 2–3 questions.
7. **Last 30 minutes before interview:** re-read only the *Results*. Walk in leading with outcomes.
